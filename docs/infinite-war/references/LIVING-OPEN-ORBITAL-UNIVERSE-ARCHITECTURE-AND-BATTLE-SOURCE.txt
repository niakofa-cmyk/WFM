### B. Friendly AI reinforcement system

This is essential.

I recommend a 20% AI composition target for a five-ship friendly combat group.

One AI pilot fills the fifth position in a five-ship formation. When more humans arrive, the formation can transition to human-controlled positions or expand according to the encounter's capacity.

The AI must not just fly alongside you.

It needs a real combat brain:

* Detect hostile fleets and incoming fire.

* Identify which enemy ships threaten its assigned human pilot.

* Select appropriate targets.

* Pursue enemies using the same 3D Orbital flight simulation.

* Acquire weapon locks and fire.

* Deploy countermeasures.

* Protect damaged friendly pilots.

* Coordinate with other AI ships.

* Destroy enemies and report battle outcomes.

* Return to formation or pursue remaining hostiles.

I would give each AI pilot a tactical role:

* Interceptor: engages enemy fighters.

* Defender: protects the player and allied ships.

* Striker: attacks vulnerable or high-value targets.

* Heavy assault: concentrates fire on capital ships.

* Support: provides defensive and tactical assistance.

The AI must be capable of independently winning an engagement, not just contributing decorative movement.

### C. Human multiplayer reinforcement

A human pilot should be able to discover your battle and join it without having to restart the operation.

The flow should be:

* Battle already underway

    You and your friendly AI are fighting two enemy fleets.

* Another pilot discovers the encounter

    Their navigation system detects the active friendly engagement.

* Server authorizes joining

    The system checks faction, encounter capacity, account, and current battle state.

* Reinforcement enters the battlefield

    The pilot receives a valid 3D approach position and joins the existing encounter.

* Shared combat continues

    Both human pilots and the AI wingmen fight the same enemies using the same authoritative simulation.

* Battle is resolved

    The server records damage, kills, assists, surviving ships, and encounter consequences.

A late-arriving pilot must not receive a separate copy of the enemy fleet. They must join the same authoritative encounter state.

### 5\. Improve flight dynamics and combat aerodynamics

That is a legitimate 3D flight foundation.

One important physics distinction: spacecraft in vacuum do not experience ordinary aerodynamic drag or lift. We should model thrust, inertia, rotational dynamics, and momentum in space. Atmospheric aerodynamics should become active when flying through the Martian atmosphere.

### Make combat feel seamless

The player should be able to:

* Turn 360 degrees without artificial directional restrictions.

* Execute barrel rolls while maintaining momentum.

* Accelerate past enemy formations.

* Perform high-speed evasive turns.

* Reverse thrust and reposition.

* Climb or descend during engagements.

* Fire while maneuvering.

* Transition from pursuit to defensive flight without switching game modes.

The key is to keep one authoritative flight simulation. The client predicts and renders movement; the server validates and resolves it.

Do not allow visual animations, camera effects, or client-side interpolation to change the actual combat outcome.

A major architectural requirement is that the current process-local session maps must eventually be replaced with distributed session ownership and durable world state.

I would use:

* PostgreSQL for persistent universe, fleet, encounter, and progression records.

* Redis or an existing shared broker for real-time coordination, messaging, and spatial ownership leases.

* Orbital simulation workers for authoritative regional simulation.

* Server snapshots and event records for recovery.

* Spatial interest management so clients only receive nearby combat actors.

Redis should not be the sole permanent record of the universe.

This gives us one shared world without requiring every player and every ship in the galaxy to run through one overloaded server process.

There are three blockers I would address before expanding the universe:

1\. Session separation. The new runtime creates sessions from saved player locations. Two players at different location keys do not automatically share the same simulation. We need seamless spatial-region transitions.

2\. Combat authority. The existing Orbital weapons library is present, but the new free-flight runtime does not yet invoke its weapon and damage functions.

3\. Persistence and scaling. The new runtime stores active actors in process memory. A server restart clears them. We need durable state and coordinated regional simulation.

There is also no verified CI pass or deployment for PR #17 yet. Its feature flag remains disabled by default.
