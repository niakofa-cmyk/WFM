# 1. ABSOLUTE ENGINE DECISION — ORBITAL EXCLUSIVELY

The Orbital Combat Engine is the sole engine for the main WAR FOR MARS game.

This is an explicit architectural requirement.

Do not use Three.js as:
- The main game engine.
- A secondary rendering engine.
- A planetary rendering engine.
- A separate universe application.
- A parallel simulation or physics runtime.
- A second world-state owner.
- A fallback gameplay runtime.

Do not build a Three.js universe and connect it to Orbital.

Do not retain Three.js as a rendering layer in the main game.

Do not create a hybrid Orbital/Three.js architecture.

**All main game functionality must operate through the Orbital engine.**

The Orbital engine must own or internally provide the complete capabilities necessary to run the game:

1. Game runtime and lifecycle.
2. World and universe management.
3. 3D rendering and visual presentation.
4. Planetary rendering and environmental systems.
5. Terrain, orbital environments, and celestial bodies.
6. Flight physics and spacecraft movement.
7. Combat, weapons, targeting, and damage.
8. AI, fleets, and encounters.
9. Coordinate systems and world navigation.
10. Streaming, LOD, and origin rebasing.
11. Camera, effects, animation, and scene management.
12. Multiplayer simulation integration.
13. World events, operations, and adventure systems.

React may remain the application and interface framework for menus, account screens, hangar interfaces, HUD interfaces, and other application-level components. It must not become another 3D game engine.

Existing server services may continue to provide authenticated identity, authoritative multiplayer validation, persistence, progression, and rewards. They must integrate with the Orbital runtime through explicit, versioned contracts.

### Three.js retirement and knowledge extraction

Treat the existing Three.js universe implementation as a research and extraction source only.

Conduct a complete inventory of its:
- Planet generation and planetary visualization.
- Sphere and surface geometry.
- Terrain and procedural detail.
- Atmosphere and lighting.
- Day/night transitions.
- Orbital paths and celestial motion.
- Camera behavior.
- Navigation and coordinate calculations.
- Ship graphics and environmental assets.
- Level of detail and rendering optimizations.
- Streaming and resource management.
- Effects and visual techniques.

Determine which concepts, algorithms, assets, and techniques are valuable.

Reimplement or adapt the useful capabilities into Orbital-native systems.

Remove the Three.js dependency from the main game only after the Orbital-native replacements are implemented, tested, and proven.

Do not preserve Three.js merely because the current Arena imports it. The purpose of this project is to replace that dependency with the Orbital engine's own complete implementation.

Do not delete useful reference code or assets before documenting and migrating the required capabilities.

# 4. REIMAGINE MARS AS ONE CONTINUOUS PLAYABLE PLANET

Mars is the first complete planetary theater and the central starting point of the Infinite War universe.

It must become a real navigable world, not a sphere placed in the background of a combat arena.

The player must be able to fly around Mars, cross the daylight hemisphere, enter the dark side, continue around the planet, and return to daylight without a map change or artificial level transition.

### Mars world layers

Build the world as connected systems:

1. Planetary body and curvature.
2. Planet-fixed geographic coordinates.
3. Body-centered inertial/orbital coordinates.
4. Surface and terrain regions.
5. Atmospheric flight envelope.
6. Low, medium, and high orbital regions.
7. Phobos and Deimos destinations.
8. Stations, shipyards, relays, and defense platforms.
9. Debris fields, wrecks, and asteroid regions.
10. Bases, installations, landing sites, and hidden locations.
11. Dynamic encounters and faction activity.
12. Persistent discoveries and world events.

A rendered globe does not count as a playable planet.

Clearly distinguish:
- Visually represented locations.
- Flyover-enabled locations.
- Collision-enabled locations.
- Landing-enabled locations.
- Fully interactive destinations.

Do not claim full planetary exploration until the actual world systems support it.

# 11. REQUIRED TESTING AND ACCEPTANCE GATES

The developer must provide evidence, not claims.

### Engine exclusivity gate
- No Three.js imports in the main game runtime.
- No Three.js rendering dependency in the production game bundle.
- No separate Three.js universe application.
- Orbital owns the game world and simulation lifecycle.
- All primary gameplay functions run through Orbital.

### Rendering gate
- Ships and environments render through Orbital.
- Planetary globe and atmosphere render through Orbital.
- Lighting and day/night operate through Orbital.
- Camera and effects operate through Orbital.
- Resource disposal and scene teardown pass.
- No duplicate rendering or simulation ownership.

### Planet gate
- Mars rotation is continuous.
- Day/night is synchronized.
- Circumnavigation works.
- Sector transitions preserve player state.
- The dark-side destination is physically navigable.
- No artificial map transition interrupts flight.

### Multiplayer gate
- Two authenticated pilots share an authoritative sector.
- Both receive consistent world and event state.
- Reconnection restores valid state.
- Combat and mission results are server-validated.

### Performance gate
Measure on defined supported devices:
- Frame-time consistency.
- CPU and GPU utilization.
- Peak memory.
- Thermal behavior.
- Startup time.
- Streaming stalls.
- Terrain and asset-loading behavior.
- Sustained combat and flight.

Do not claim 60 FPS or mobile certification without physical-device evidence.

### Regression gate
- Existing account and authentication contracts remain valid.
- Existing reward protections remain valid.
- Existing campaign and co-op behavior remains accounted for.
- No duplicate inventory or progression systems.
- Rollback is documented and tested.
