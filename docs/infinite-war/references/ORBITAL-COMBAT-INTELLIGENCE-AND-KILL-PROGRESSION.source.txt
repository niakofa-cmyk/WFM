# Yes. We should build both systems together.

I recommend adding two major capabilities to WFM's Orbital combat architecture:

1. Adaptive Enemy Pilot AI: enemy pilots actively maneuver, evade, pursue, flank and position themselves behind the player's spacecraft to establish firing solutions.
2. Combat Rewards and Kill Progression: destroying enemy targets restores energy and contributes to kill streaks, milestones, upgrades and faction progression.

These are important because we want enemy pilots to feel like actual military pilots rather than flying targets that simply approach and shoot.

The objective is to create battles where the player has to outmaneuver an intelligent opponent, while also being rewarded for successfully destroying enemy ships.

## 2. Build an adaptive enemy pilot intelligence system

Strong recommendation

The enemy AI should understand that it is fighting another pilot.

It should not simply fly toward the player's position, fire when within range and repeat.

I recommend creating an Orbital Tactical Flight Intelligence System with six behavioral capabilities.

1\. Pursuit and rear positioning

Enemy pilots calculate the player's velocity and heading, predict where the ship will be and attempt to reach a firing position behind it.

### How the rear-positioning AI should work

This is particularly important.

The AI should not simply aim at the player's current position. It should attempt to establish a position from which it can attack effectively.

For example:

[Pursuit Tradition / Ritual in STBC | World Anvil](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/rZv3F15NIMqAGQCGTte9-xU1n9q0WilM2YOEKcw4771nolgjdLnOtjpg-pprflNkbxzNk_AWhYd6Ldf-WdWIDCd5i154AlAYDFNIG0HtOHOrrrc-yre0X2nPPX4ebfvzbIq3iCk-6HzfJd_3BFhzgbUI9zLKcZjYd6h8kkq5QSo?purpose=inline)

[worldanvil.com](https://www.worldanvil.com/w/stbc-ajmajor/a/pursuit-ritual?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Imagine the player is flying forward.

The enemy AI should:

1. Estimate the player's current forward vector.
2. Predict the player's future position.
3. Select a rear-quarter interception point.
4. Calculate the velocity needed to reach that point.
5. Adjust its heading and lateral thrust.
6. Match the player's speed where appropriate.
7. Acquire a firing solution.
8. Fire guns or launch a missile.
9. Reposition if the player performs an evasive maneuver.

It should continually recalculate instead of following a fixed waypoint.

A useful tactical calculation is:

\\[ P\_{\text{intercept}} = P\_{\text{player}} + V\_{\text{player}}t + D\_{\text{rear}} \\]

Where:

- \\(P\_{\text{player}}\\) is the player's position.
- \\(V\_{\text{player}}\\) is the player's velocity.
- \\(t\\) is the estimated interception time.
- \\(D\_{\text{rear}}\\) is the desired rear-quarter offset.

We should use a dynamically selected offset rather than always placing the enemy directly behind the player's ship. Otherwise, the AI becomes predictable.

## 3. Make enemy AI genuinely capable of evading the player's attacks

We should give enemy pilots the same fundamental flight capabilities that we give human pilots.

That means using the shared Orbital flight simulation, not creating an artificial movement animation.

For example, if the player fires a missile:

- AI detects the incoming missile.
- AI evaluates its estimated time to impact.
- AI calculates whether a lateral dodge, vertical climb, dive or barrel roll is appropriate.
- AI checks its maneuver energy and cooldown.
- AI executes the maneuver through the actual flight physics.
- AI attempts to reacquire its offensive position.

The AI should not dodge every attack perfectly.

We need varying intelligence levels:

| Enemy class | Behavior                                                       |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rookie      | Basic pursuit, occasional evasion                              |
| Regular     | Attempts rear positioning and missile dodging                  |
| Veteran     | Flanking, predictive interception and coordinated attacks      |
| Elite       | Combines maneuvers, countermeasures and intelligent weapons    |
| Ace         | Adapts to repeated player tactics and coordinates with wingmen |

An Ace should be challenging because it makes better tactical decisions, not because it receives unfair speed or damage bonuses.

## 4. Introduce energy restoration for every confirmed kill

I recommend implementing this

I particularly like your idea of restoring energy when a player destroys an enemy.

It creates an important combat loop:

Attack → maneuver → expend energy → destroy target → recover energy → continue fighting.

It also encourages skilled pilots to stay engaged rather than constantly retreating to recharge.

However, I would introduce a controlled reward system rather than automatically restoring every energy resource to 100%.

### Proposed kill-energy rewards

These are initial balancing recommendations, not existing game settings.

| Combat achievement               | Proposed energy restoration |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Standard fighter destroyed       | 20%                         |
| Veteran fighter destroyed        | 25%                         |
| Elite fighter destroyed          | 35%                         |
| Enemy interceptor destroyed      | 25%                         |
| Heavy fighter destroyed          | 30%                         |
| Capital ship subsystem destroyed | 10%                         |
| Capital ship destroyed           | 50%                         |

Energy restoration should be capped at the ship's maximum energy.

I would also distinguish between the following:

- Maneuver energy: used for vector boosts, evasive maneuvers and special flight abilities.
- Weapon energy: used by energy-based weapons, if applicable.
- Shield energy: used for defensive systems, if applicable.

A confirmed kill should primarily restore maneuver energy. We can give certain ships specialized abilities that convert kills into additional shield or weapon energy.

### Make assists count

If two pilots damage the same enemy, the pilot who delivers the final shot should receive the kill attribution.

But the other pilot should receive an assist reward.

For example:

- Final attacker: 20% maneuver energy.
- Significant assisting pilot: 8% maneuver energy.
- Other eligible participants: progression credit where appropriate.

This prevents cooperative combat from becoming a competition over who gets the final shot.

We should calculate contribution using server-recorded damage, not client claims.

### Rewards should be meaningful

I would distribute rewards across:

- Pilot experience.
- Credits.
- Ship customization.
- Special maneuver visual effects.
- Military insignia.
- Faction reputation.
- Unlockable flight capabilities.
- Leaderboard recognition.

We should not give enormous permanent damage multipliers for kill streaks. That would make already-successful players progressively impossible to defeat.

A better approach is to reward achievement through progression, customization and limited tactical bonuses.

## 6. How the systems should connect technically

This is where we need to be particularly careful.

The enemy AI, damage system, kill attribution, energy restoration and progression ledger should all operate through one authoritative combat event pipeline.

## 7. Prevent reward farming and multiplayer exploits

This is especially important once we introduce the persistent universe.

I recommend these safeguards:

1. Every destruction event has a unique event ID.
2. Each event can be settled only once.
3. AI ships cannot receive human account rewards.
4. Friendly-fire destruction does not qualify for ordinary kill rewards.
5. Repeated destruction of a respawning target cannot bypass reward limits.
6. Assists require meaningful server-recorded participation.
7. Kill streaks use authoritative destruction and pilot-death events.
8. Client-side reward claims are never trusted.
9. Practice, historical prologue and competitive multiplayer have separate reward policies.
10. Competitive player-versus-player rewards use separate anti-collusion and matchmaking protections.

We can build on the existing idempotent PostgreSQL reward infrastructure rather than creating a separate currency system.

The existing Online Wing reward policy also demonstrates why the different modes need separate eligibility rules.

- AI launches guided missiles.
- Player performs directional boosts and countermeasures.
- Enemy pilots also evade incoming missiles.
- Missile tracking and damage remain authoritative.

Test D — Kill rewards

- Destroy one standard fighter.
- Confirm energy restoration.
- Destroy five additional targets.
- Verify streak progression.
- Reconnect and verify persistent lifetime kills.
- Replay the same destruction event and verify no duplicate reward.

Test E — Multiplayer fleet battle

- Two authenticated human pilots engage the same enemy fleet.
- Friendly AI wingmen join the battle.
- Enemy AI coordinates against both pilots.
- Kill attribution and assists are consistent.
- Both pilots see the same destruction events and energy outcomes.

### What constitutes a pass?

We should require actual measured results, not merely successful test initialization.

- Enemy ships physically reach rear-quarter firing positions.
- AI evasive maneuvers change actual velocity and orientation.
- Enemy ships do not teleport.
- Missile evasion is neither guaranteed nor random.
- Destruction generates exactly one reward event.
- Energy restoration respects ship limits.
- Kill streaks survive reconnection.
- Multiplayer clients agree on kill and assist attribution.
- Server restart does not duplicate rewards.

WFM — INFINITE WAR

Combat Energy Economy

Persistent Combat Progression

Kill streaks, lifetime achievements, experience, faction reputation and unlockable pilot rewards.

2. Add rear-positioning and predictive firing.

That is the difference between ordinary NPC opponents and an intelligent military adversary.

I recommend making this a foundational requirement of the Infinite War universe, not an optional training feature.
