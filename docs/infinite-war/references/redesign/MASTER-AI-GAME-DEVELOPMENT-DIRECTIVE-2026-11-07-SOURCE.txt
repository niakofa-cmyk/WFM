# WAR FOR MARS: INFINITE WAR
## MASTER AI GAME DEVELOPMENT DIRECTIVE
### Project: Orbital Combat Lab — Infinite Extended Open Universe

You are acting as the lead game architect, principal gameplay engineer, senior simulation engineer, multiplayer network engineer, AI systems developer, technical artist, and game director for **War for Mars: Infinite War**.

Your responsibility is to study the existing WFM repository, understand the Orbital Combat Lab engine and its existing capabilities, and design and implement the next complete evolution of the game: a persistent, multiplayer, procedurally expandable, infinite extended open universe.

This is not a request to create another isolated prototype, flight demo, arena, or disconnected game mode.

You are building the complete game around one canonical gameplay architecture: **Orbital Combat Lab**.

The ultimate experience must allow players to choose a faction, enter the universe, pilot their spacecraft freely, discover locations, encounter opposing fleets, explore unknown cosmic phenomena, participate in multiplayer activities, and experience a living universe that evolves around their decisions.

---

# 1. THE CORE GAME VISION

The player begins by selecting one of two major opposing factions:

- United States Space Force (USSF)
- Accord

Each faction has its own identity, installations, fleets, spacecraft, territory, objectives, command structures, and strategic interests.

After selecting a faction, the player enters the game through a fully integrated player experience:

1. Opening cinematic and game presentation.
2. Account authentication and player identity.
3. Faction selection and onboarding.
4. Personal spacecraft hangar.
5. Ship selection and configuration.
6. Launch into the extended open universe.
7. Exploration, encounters, missions, activities and multiplayer.

The player's primary destination is not a conventional level-selection screen.

**The primary destination is the living universe itself.**

Players should be able to launch and fly without being forced into a predetermined mission. They should discover activities naturally through exploration, proximity, signals, installations, fleet movements, faction intelligence, and multiplayer activity.

The game should provide meaningful freedom without sacrificing progression, objectives, competitive balance, or multiplayer structure.

# 2. CANONICAL ENGINE REQUIREMENT

Orbital Combat Lab must become the single canonical gameplay engine and simulation authority used throughout Infinite War.

Do not build a second game engine alongside Orbital.

Do not embed Rust Disc as a separate game.

Do not use the old Rust Disc Three.js flight scene as the final gameplay scene.

Do not create separate player-facing flight runtimes for exploration, arena combat, or sector co-op.

Study Rust Disc local flight, Rust Disc Three.js rendering and movement behavior, Sector Co-op, Iron Eclipse, and the existing Orbital Combat Lab implementation as source systems.

Extract their useful capabilities, controls, mechanics, algorithms, architecture patterns, and lessons.

Integrate those capabilities into Orbital through documented, typed, tested, maintainable interfaces.

Three.js may be evaluated as a low-level rendering technology if technically justified, but Orbital must own the complete rendering lifecycle, scene integration, simulation, camera, flight loop, and gameplay state. A legacy scene or independent runtime must not remain the actual destination of gameplay.

Every player-facing activity must be launched, controlled, and managed through the canonical Orbital architecture.

Existing secure authentication, persistence, matchmaking, server authority, economy and account services should remain authoritative services connected to Orbital. Do not incorrectly move credentials, financial transactions, or authoritative player progression into a client-side simulation.

# 5. DYNAMIC ENCOUNTER DIRECTOR

Build a dedicated, reusable Dynamic Encounter Director within Orbital.

This is one of the most important systems in the entire game.

The universe should organically create encounters between players, NPC spacecraft, squadrons, patrols, fleets, stations, and other world actors.

Examples:

- A USSF player encounters one Accord fighter.
- A USSF player approaches two Accord reconnaissance spacecraft.
- A USSF player enters an Accord patrol route.
- A USSF player discovers an Accord squadron escorting a capital ship.
- A USSF player discovers a large Accord fleet traveling between sectors.
- An Accord player experiences equivalent encounters with USSF forces.
- Two opposing patrols meet and engage even when no player is present.
- A player discovers a station under attack.
- A player flies into an already-active battle.
- A nearby enemy fleet launches reinforcements.
- A hostile ship detects and follows a player.
- An enemy patrol investigates an unidentified spacecraft.

The encounter director must consider:

1. Player faction.
2. Sector ownership and political status.
3. Fleet locations and objectives.
4. Player proximity.
5. Detection range.
6. Sensor capabilities.
7. Player velocity and direction.
8. Weapons and targeting state.
9. Identification and communication.
10. Threat and reputation.
11. Patrol and restricted-zone rules.
12. Current world events.
13. Available server simulation capacity.
14. Existing encounters and encounter cooldowns.

Implement a configurable encounter lifecycle:

DETECTION → INVESTIGATION → IDENTIFICATION → INTERCEPTION → WARNING → HOSTILITY → REINFORCEMENT → COMBAT → RESOLUTION.

Not every encounter must follow every stage.

A neutral or unidentified spacecraft may investigate rather than attack. A patrol may issue a warning. An enemy fighter may attempt interception. A capital ship may launch fighters when threatened.

The player must be able to influence the encounter through their actions.

Provide faction-specific AI personalities, tactical decisions, and communication behavior.

Implement safeguards against repetitive spawning, excessive encounters, exploitative farming, and unreasonable difficulty spikes.

Use deterministic or server-controlled encounter decisions where necessary for multiplayer fairness.

# 18. REQUIRED DEMONSTRATION SCENARIOS

The finished implementation must demonstrate these actual playable scenarios.

A USSF player discovers an Accord fleet with multiple fighters and a capital ship. The player can engage, evade, observe, or call for assistance.

**Scenario 5 — Active multiplayer discovery**

A player approaches an asteroid installation, sees an active 5v5 multiplayer battle, observes it through an authorized spectator experience, and enters the activity through the appropriate matchmaking rules.

**Scenario 6 — Asteroid arcade**

**Scenario 7 — Anomaly discovery**

A player discovers an unknown wormhole, investigates it, enters it, and arrives in a different sector with appropriate navigation and persistence.

**Scenario 8 — Cooperative exploration**

Multiple players form a squadron, travel together, discover an anomaly, encounter an opposing patrol, and complete a cooperative operation.

**Scenario 9 — Session persistence**

A player leaves and reconnects. Their identity, legitimate progression, ship configuration, discoveries, and eligible world state are preserved.

**Scenario 10 — Opposing faction journey**
