4. Critical discovery: Orbital still needs hull-specific special-ability integration
This is the most important technical finding in this audit.

The repository contains all eight special-ability definitions and a triggerUlt() implementation in the existing simulation.

However, the canonical Orbital execution path has a gap.

In artifacts/api-server/src/game/sim.ts, the simulation dispatches spatial craft to stepOrbital() and immediately returns. The legacy triggerUlt() function is below that branch and is used by the non-spatial flight path.

The Orbital stepOrbital() implementation does not currently call the hull-specific ability handler.

That means the presence of a special-ability name, cooldown display or HUD control does not prove that the actual ability works in Orbital.
Priority architecture gap
The eight existing abilities need to be migrated into the Orbital simulation itself.

This is especially important because the missile system already has a canonical implementation:
- Missile range: 65.
- Lock duration: 1.25 seconds.
- Missile cooldown: 3.5 seconds.
- Player ammunition: 8.
- Player missile damage: 58 in the current launch path.
- AI missile damage: 16 in that path.
- Guided missile steering and collision handling.
The missile system is functional at the shared simulation level, but it is largely a common missile type. It is not yet a sophisticated per-ship missile arsenal.

The shared FlightInput also does not currently declare a general countermeasure input. Therefore, the flare behavior described in the latest update should not yet be assumed to be a complete, reusable defensive system across all ships and game modes.

First: the static ship illustrations.

ShipIllustration.tsx contains eight distinct SVG hull silhouettes. The tests verify that all eight have different geometry, correct ship identity and faction colors.

It also provides special geometry for the Tripura and Splitter rings.

7. The bigger opportunity: fleet warfare, not just more spacecraft
The real breakthrough is giving each ship a job within a larger fleet.

Imagine a USSF player flying through the Cinder Ring and discovering a large Accord fleet.

Instead of encountering five identical enemies, they encounter:
- Reconnaissance craft detecting them.
- Interceptors moving to intercept.
- Bombers approaching from a different vector.
- Missile destroyers launching guided salvos.
- Escort ships protecting a capital vessel.
- A carrier deploying additional fighters.
- A command cruiser coordinating the engagement.
That is a completely different experience.

9. The development sequence I would actually choose
I would avoid asking the AI to build sixteen ships, twenty missile types and capital-ship warfare in one enormous implementation pass.

That creates a serious risk of impressive-looking but disconnected systems.

My preferred sequence is:
Fleet expansion roadmap
1. First: finish the existing eight ships
   
   Wire all eight special abilities into Orbital, verify their weapons, missiles, handling and actual 3D construction.
   Foundation priority
2. Second: build the weapon and missile architecture
   
   Introduce differentiated launchers, guidance, countermeasures, ammunition, hardpoints and ship loadouts.
3. Third: add four USSF and four Accord spacecraft
   
   Complete the sixteen-ship player roster, with distinctive hulls and specialized combat capabilities.
4. Fourth: introduce capital ships and fleet formations
   
   Add AI-controlled carriers, destroyers, cruisers and dreadnoughts, together with coordinated tactical formations.
5. Fifth: integrate autonomous fleet warfare into the universe
   
   Allow players to discover moving fleets, encounter patrols, join battles and participate in large-scale faction operations.

10. The most important design decision
I would make one feature the centerpiece of this expansion:

The player should be able to encounter an entire military fleet, not merely individual enemy fighters.

Imagine flying as USSF through an unexplored sector and detecting an Accord carrier group. The carrier is deploying fighters. Interceptors begin pursuing you. A bomber squadron begins lining up a strike. The destroyer launches missiles. Your wingmates take defensive positions.

You can retreat, attack the escorts, target the carrier's hangar, call reinforcements, or attempt to destroy the fleet's command vessel.

That is the type of experience that can distinguish Infinite War.

And it gives the new ship fleet a genuine purpose beyond simply offering more vehicles to select in the hangar.

My strongest recommendation is to complete the existing eight Orbital ships and their abilities before introducing the additional eight. That gives the developer a stable foundation for the military fleet expansion rather than multiplying incomplete systems.
