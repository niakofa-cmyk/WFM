# War for Mars: Infinite War — The Infinite Open Universe

I think this is a very strong direction for War for Mars. More importantly, I think you've identified something that could make the game substantially different from a traditional space-combat game.

The game should not feel like a collection of missions that you select from a menu. It should feel like an enormous, living universe that you enter, explore, encounter, and participate in.

You choose your allegiance, USSF or the Accord, enter the universe, launch your spacecraft, and decide where to go. You might spend 30 minutes exploring an asteroid field, discover a military installation, encounter an enemy patrol, investigate a mysterious anomaly, or stumble upon an active multiplayer battle.

The universe becomes the game itself. The arcade skirmish, competitive multiplayer, fleet engagements, exploration, and other game modes become activities that exist inside that universe.

I would absolutely pursue this direction.

I would build the game around five connected experiences.

### A. The infinite exploration universe

Core game experience

The player is not simply choosing a level. They are piloting a spacecraft through a continuously explorable universe.

They can:

- Fly between planets, moons, asteroid fields, orbital installations, and deep-space territories.
- Discover hidden military bases, abandoned stations, mining colonies, shipyards, and research facilities.
- Encounter other players and their squadrons.
- Discover USSF and Accord fleets traveling through the universe.
- Find distress signals, unknown transmissions, abandoned vessels, and unexplored locations.
- Enter wormholes, black holes, dimensional rifts, and other celestial anomalies.
- Discover previously unknown sectors and routes.
- Encounter multiplayer activities while exploring rather than having to leave the universe to access them.

The universe should have a sense of scale. Flying away from Mars should lead somewhere meaningful, not simply to another disconnected arena.

Imagine selecting USSF.

You launch from a USSF installation and fly beyond the immediate orbital territory.

You encounter:

1. A single Accord reconnaissance spacecraft.
2. Two Accord fighters traveling together.
3. An Accord patrol squadron.
4. A large Accord fleet traveling through a sector.
5. An Accord capital ship accompanied by fighter escorts.
6. A hostile fleet attacking a neutral station.
7. A large-scale engagement involving multiple player squadrons.

These should not all immediately attack you.

The encounter system should evaluate:

- Your distance from the opposing ships.
- Whether their sensors detect you.
- Your speed and flight direction.
- Whether you are transmitting an identification signal.
- Your faction and reputation.
- Whether you have entered a restricted territory.
- Whether you have weapons activated or are targeting them.
- Whether the fleet is already engaged in another operation.

A reconnaissance ship might investigate you. A patrol might issue a warning. A hostile squadron might intercept you. A capital fleet might launch fighters.

Your actions should influence the encounters.

The same system works in reverse for Accord players encountering USSF forces.

This creates an important distinction: the universe isn't simply populated with enemies. It is populated with intelligent, faction-driven actors that have their own objectives.

This is where I would give the game its most distinctive exploration identity.

Possible discoveries include:

- Black holes with gravitational effects on spacecraft.
- Wormholes connecting distant sectors.
- Unstable dimensional rifts.
- Ancient alien structures.
- Abandoned megastructures.
- Uncharted planetary systems.
- Time-distortion regions.
- Dangerous energy storms.
- Unknown civilizations.
- Lost fleets and derelict capital ships.

These should not merely be visual effects.

An anomaly should have gameplay consequences: navigation interference, altered gravity, unusual enemy behavior, special resources, hidden routes, or unique multiplayer expeditions.

### E. A universe that continues evolving

The universe should feel active even when a player isn't looking at it.

Fleets travel. Stations change ownership. Patrols investigate signals. Squadrons conduct operations. Battles occur. Players discover locations. Factions respond to threats.

This requires a persistent universe simulation, not simply randomly spawning objects around the player.

## 2. How I would architect the new game

I would make one important architectural decision before development begins:

Orbital Combat Lab becomes the single canonical engine that powers the entire Infinite War universe.

We should not build a separate exploration game, a separate flight game, and a separate multiplayer game that happen to share a title. We should build one engine capable of hosting all those experiences.

The encounter director might evaluate:

1. What sector is the player entering?
2. Which faction controls the sector?
3. Which fleets and ships are currently operating there?
4. What is the player's faction?
5. Which encounters are already taking place?
6. What is the player's current threat level?
7. Which encounter would make sense given the current world state?

It could then create an encounter with a single enemy fighter, a patrol, a squadron, or a much larger fleet.

The system should support escalating stages:

1. Detection

   Enemy sensors detect a possible unidentified spacecraft.
2. Investigation

   A reconnaissance ship or patrol changes its course to investigate.
3. Interception

   The enemy approaches, scans, challenges or attempts to identify the player.
4. Hostility

   The encounter becomes combat because of faction rules, player actions or enemy orders.
5. Reinforcement

   Nearby ships, fighters or capital vessels may respond.
6. Resolution

   Ships retreat, surrender, are destroyed, complete their objective or call for assistance.

This should be a reusable simulation system that works for both factions, NPCs and multiplayer encounters.

## 4. How to make the universe feel genuinely enormous

There is a technical distinction worth getting right: an infinitely explorable universe does not mean the game must simulate an infinite number of objects simultaneously.

I recommend a seeded, procedurally expandable universe with persistent discovered content.

The universe can generate new sectors deterministically as players explore, using stable coordinates, seeds and generation rules. Important discoveries, stations, player-created changes and major battles can be saved to authoritative world data.

That gives us:

- An expandable universe without a predefined final boundary.
- Consistent locations when players revisit a sector.
- The ability for multiple players to discover the same locations.
- Persistent points of interest and world events.
- Efficient streaming of nearby sectors.
- Large distances without requiring every distant ship to be simulated in real time.

For performance, nearby spacecraft receive detailed simulation. Distant fleets can use lower-frequency strategic simulation. Unoccupied regions can use background state updates rather than running every spacecraft at full fidelity.

The goal is an effectively unlimited world with intelligent simulation—not an unnecessarily expensive simulation of everything at once.

## 6. My recommendations for making this vision exceptional

There are three things I would emphasize beyond the prompt.

First: make discovery exciting. Don't tell players everything that exists. Let them find signals, unknown installations, unexplored systems, and events. Give exploration real rewards and consequences.

Second: make encounters unpredictable but believable. Randomness should be governed by faction intelligence, location, threat, and world activity. A universe that constantly spawns enemies becomes repetitive. A universe with believable patrols, investigations, reinforcements, and conflicts feels alive.

Third: make multiplayer part of the world, not a separate destination. The asteroid competitive game should remain. But the universe should become the framework through which players discover it, watch it, join it, and return to exploration.

I would prioritize development in this order:

1. Universe foundation: Create the navigable, expandable universe and sector streaming.
2. Flight experience: Integrate the Rust Disc pilot controls into Orbital.
3. Faction intelligence: Populate the universe with USSF and Accord installations, patrols and fleets.
4. Dynamic encounters: Make enemy detection, investigation, interception and combat work.
5. Multiplayer discovery: Connect the existing asteroid arena and competitive modes to the universe.
6. Anomaly exploration: Add wormholes, black holes and dimensional discoveries.
7. Complete player experience: Rebuild the frontend, hangar, onboarding, navigation and settings around the universe.

I would not start by creating hundreds of planets or spacecraft. I would first build a small but fully functioning universe demonstrating the entire gameplay loop, then expand its scale and content.

My biggest recommendation: Make the first milestone a playable vertical slice in which a USSF player launches from Mars, flies through an asteroid sector, discovers an Accord patrol, triggers an encounter, fights or escapes, discovers a multiplayer asteroid installation, and enters the existing 5v5 activity before returning to the universe.

If that works seamlessly, you have demonstrated the central concept of Infinite War.

And that is the direction I would pursue: not just a space shooter with an open-world mode, but an entire universe in which the space shooter, exploration, faction warfare, and multiplayer experiences coexist.
