I strongly recommend that we build a complete weapon acquisition, salvage, exploration, and visible-mount system for WFM—not a system where players only receive weapons by destroying enemy ships.

Your idea of finding weapons, energy, and useful resources throughout the universe is exactly the direction I would take. It gives players a reason to explore, fight, investigate derelict locations, and search dangerous regions rather than repeatedly fighting enemies just to obtain better equipment.

I would combine the weapon acquisition philosophy of Einhänder with a much deeper persistent-universe economy.

2\. Asteroid and planetary discoveries

Discover abandoned weapon caches, buried technology, rare energy crystals, ancient weapons, experimental components, and forgotten installations.

3\. Derelict ships, stations, and ancient installations

Find abandoned military vessels, destroyed outposts, forgotten research facilities, and hidden storage chambers.

These locations can contain weapons that cannot normally be obtained from ordinary enemies.

I would also add mission rewards, elite commanders, boss encounters, faction research, and weapon manufacturing as additional acquisition methods.

The principle: combat earns salvage, exploration discovers technology, and progression unlocks specialization.

The salvage system should consider:

| Component           | Possible result                         |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Main cannons        | Recoverable weapon or damaged component |
| Missile launchers   | Launcher module or ammunition           |
| Defensive turrets   | Salvageable turret                      |
| Energy core         | Power source or crafting material       |
| Armor               | Alloy and reinforcement materials       |
| Experimental weapon | Rare technology, if it survives         |
| Hull                | Structural materials                    |

There should be several possible outcomes:

* Intact: weapon can be recovered and equipped.

* Damaged: requires repair before use.

* Destroyed: only components or materials remain.

* Volatile: dangerous energy or ammunition may explode if approached carelessly.

This creates an interesting tactical decision: do you destroy an enemy immediately, or disable its weapon systems and attempt to preserve valuable equipment?

Every weapon should have a physical model, attachment position, orientation, firing animation, muzzle effect, recoil, projectile origin, and damage behavior.

When a player changes weapons in the hangar:

* The old weapon physically detaches.

* The new weapon moves into the appropriate hardpoint.

### Hull compatibility is essential

Not every weapon should fit every ship.

Each ship should define:

* Hardpoint positions.

* Hardpoint size.

* Weapon categories allowed.

* Maximum weapon mass.

* Power capacity.

* Heat capacity.

* Weapon firing arc.

* Mount restrictions.

That gives different ships meaningful equipment identities. A small interceptor should not carry the same weapon configuration as a heavy assault ship.

| Discovery             | What players can obtain                              |

| Asteroids             | Minerals, alloys, rare crystals                      |

Importantly, not every asteroid should contain a weapon.

### 7\. How to connect the entire system technically

I would build this around a server-authoritative architecture.

One important distinction for WFM: the existing project direction establishes Orbital Combat Lab as the canonical runtime. If Three.js is being used for the current universe's visual rendering or a legacy scene, we should integrate the visible mounts there without creating a second authoritative game runtime.

The architecture should look like this:

```mermaid

flowchart TD
    A[Enemy Destruction Event] --> B[Server Salvage Evaluator]
    C[Asteroid or Wreck Discovery] --> D[Exploration Reward Evaluator]
    E[Mission or Boss Completion] --> F[Mission Reward Evaluator]

    B --> G[Authoritative Inventory]
    D --> G
    F --> G

    G --> H[Weapon Compatibility Validator]
    H --> I[Server-Issued Loadout]
    I --> J[Ship Hardpoint Resolver]
    J --> K[Visible Weapon Mounts]
    J --> L[Combat Weapon Runtime]

    G --> M[Resource and Crafting System]
    M --> N[Repair and Upgrade]
```

The key components should be:

| System                      | Responsibility                                     |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| WeaponCatalog               | Defines every weapon and its attributes            |
| ShipHardpointDefinitions    | Defines physical mount positions and compatibility |
| LoadoutService              | Validates and persists equipped weapons            |
| SalvageService              | Determines what survives enemy destruction         |
| ExplorationDiscoveryService | Handles asteroid, wreck, and anomaly discoveries   |
| ResourceInventoryService    | Stores energy, minerals, and components            |
| WeaponMountRenderer         | Displays actual weapons on the ship                |
| WeaponCombatService         | Handles authoritative firing and damage            |
| CraftingAndRepairService    | Repairs and manufactures equipment                 |

The system must preserve provenance. If a weapon came from a particular enemy ship, asteroid cache, or research facility, its origin should remain recorded.

It should also prevent duplicate collection, repeated reward claims, and farming exploits.

### 8\. My recommended acquisition balance

I would not make all three acquisition methods equally rewarding. They should encourage different play styles.

These are starting design targets, not fixed drop probabilities. The actual balance should be tested against playtime, weapon rarity, player progression, and faction economy.

My reasoning:

* Combat-focused players can earn equipment through battles.

* Explorers can find unusual and rare equipment.

* Mission-focused players can pursue specific rewards.

* Dedicated builders can manufacture and customize their preferred loadouts.

No player should be forced to spend all their time fighting the same enemy types just to improve their ship.

### 9\. The most important addition: enemy loadout provenance

I would make this a non-negotiable requirement.

When an enemy appears in the universe, its weapons should be generated from an actual loadout.

When that enemy is destroyed, the salvage evaluator should inspect that loadout and the destruction results.

### 10\. The implementation order I would choose

I would build these in this order:

* Server-issued weapon catalog and ship-compatible hardpoints.

* Visible physical weapon mounts on every supported ship.

* Enemy-generated loadouts with weapon-to-hull provenance.

* Destruction-based salvage and recovery.

* Asteroid, wreck, and station discovery.

* Energy, ammunition, minerals, and crafting resources.

* Repair, manufacturing, upgrades, and weapon progression.

* Multiplayer synchronization and persistence across the universe.

One critical distinction: do not build the exploration loot system first and leave the visible-mount gap unresolved. Players need to see the weapons they own, and those weapons must be the exact weapons the server says they have equipped.

My final recommendation is to make WFM's weapon ecosystem a combination of Einhänder-style physical weapon attachment, enemy weapon recovery, and open-universe exploration. That would give the game a meaningful gameplay loop: discover, fight, disable, salvage, recover, equip, customize, and return to battle.

The next step I can take is to turn this into a file-specific WFM implementation directive, with acceptance tests proving that a weapon recovered from a real enemy or discovered in the universe becomes a persistent, compatible, visibly mounted, server-authorized weapon.
