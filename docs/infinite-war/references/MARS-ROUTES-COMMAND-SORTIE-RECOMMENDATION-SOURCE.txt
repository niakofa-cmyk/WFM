The important finding is that the repository currently contains several partially overlapping versions of the game's story and mission structure. There are good foundations, but they do not yet describe one unified campaign running through one Orbital-exclusive universe.

### 1. The storyline and mission chain are not fully aligned

The current campaign in `artifacts/war-for-mars/src/game/campaign.ts` has four sorties:

1. Contact — break the defending wing at Rust Disc and recover a route key.
2. Beacon — secure two of three Pale Beacons.
3. Warden — destroy the Warden on the night side.
4. Cage — intercept or escort the transport carrying Eon Dusk through Dust Lane.

That sequence has a compelling mystery: the signal predates the USSF–Accord conflict, the Warden was ordered to erase evidence, and the cage is connected to a signal beneath Mars.

But the separate co-op sector mission system in `artifacts/war-for-mars/src/game/sector-missions.ts` defines a different progression:

- Rust Disc → Mars Approach → Pale Beacons.
- Iron Eclipse is independent, with no prerequisite.

These are not yet two views of one coherent campaign. The campaign's Warden and Cage story beats are not represented in that co-op sector chain, while Iron Eclipse is not properly positioned within the narrative sequence.

### 2. The current route system is too mission-instance oriented

The sector definitions reuse `RUST_DISC_SECTOR` as their starting geometry. They assign different relay, extraction, and drone positions, but use a shared Mars position and radius.

That is useful for building and testing bounded co-op encounters. It is not yet a representation of the continuous Mars environment we envision.

For example, Mars Approach is explicitly described as an orbital survey where surface landing is not part of the flight. That is a valid current implementation boundary, but the future route design must support a much richer journey: orbital arrival, planetary circumnavigation, high and low orbit, atmospheric entry when implemented, surface destinations, stations, and onward travel.

### 3. Commands are tactical, not yet a full military command system

The current co-op command vocabulary is:

- Attack
- Defend
- Escort
- Regroup

Iron Eclipse adds orders for its three-ship AI wing and target selection among point defense, shield array, and reactor.

These are useful mechanics, but they are not yet the command structure for a military space-war game. They need to grow into a system that connects:

- Strategic command and campaign objectives.
- Fleet and squadron tasking.
- Wingman and AI behavior.
- Escort, interception, reconnaissance, rescue, suppression, defense, and extraction.
- Tactical target assignment and subsystem priorities.
- Dynamic orders that respond to what is happening in the shared world.

The distinction matters: an order should not just change a label on the HUD. Where implemented, it should cause the appropriate server-authoritative ships, objectives, and mission state to respond.

### 4. The architecture documents themselves need correction

The current `docs/infinite-war/MASTER-DEVELOPMENT-DIRECTIVE.md`, `STRUCTURAL-REBUILD-ROADMAP.md`, and `ARCHITECTURE-ASSESSMENT.md` still describe Three.js as the production runtime or presentation foundation.

That conflicts with your clarified direction. Orbital Combat Engine must be the exclusive game engine and runtime, including native rendering and simulation. React can remain the interface layer; existing backend services can remain responsible for identity, authoritative multiplayer, persistence, and progression. Three.js is reference material only, not a runtime fallback.

The storyline and mission redesign must be written against that corrected architecture, not the older renderer assumptions.

### What sortie missions should become

Instead of only offering a small set of fixed combat goals, build a reusable operation framework. Each operation should have:

- A narrative reason and issuing authority.
- A geographic location in the shared universe.
- A primary objective and optional objectives.
- A sequence of phases and conditional branches.
- Player, human squadron, and AI roles.
- Enemy composition and reinforcement rules.
- Time, distance, threat, and extraction conditions.
- Dynamic events and possible complications.
- A debrief that reports what actually happened.

4. Wing command: pilot assignments, formation behavior, and tactical objectives.
5. Tactical control: targets, subsystem priorities, engagement, defense, escort, regroup, and extraction.

Define which commands belong to the player, the operation host, AI commanders, and server-authoritative systems.

The command interface should communicate:

- Issuing authority.
- Current order.
- Assigned unit or target.
- Order status.
- Priority.
- Objective completion.
- Threat changes.
- Available alternatives.
- Consequences of abandoning or changing an order.

Do not grant a player authority they do not actually possess in the operation.

- Defend position.
- Escort convoy or vessel.
- Intercept hostile.
- Screen friendly formation.
- Reconnoiter location.
- Search and rescue.
- Suppress hostile defenses.
- Protect capital ship.
- Attack formation.
- Hold formation.
- Break formation.
- Regroup.
- Withdraw.
- Cover extraction.
- Recover or rescue damaged wingmates.

Where supported, commands must produce actual server-authoritative AI movement, target selection, combat behavior, and objective changes.

Orders should support target references, unit assignments, priority, duration, cancellation, replacement, and validation.

Human pilots retain control of their own ships. Commands coordinate them; they do not silently take over their controls.

### 11. Make the universe persistent and responsive

- A failed operation creates a new threat or recovery opportunity.
- A major fleet engagement changes the strategic situation.
- A discovery unlocks new exploration or narrative content.

These are design goals, not permission to award progress based on client claims.

Only authenticated, server-validated, idempotent results may change account progression, inventory, rewards, faction standing, or persistent world state.

Define rules for contested outcomes, simultaneous operations, faction attribution, replay protection, and recovery.
