# WAR FOR MARS: THE INFINITE WAR
## Master chronology and story canon v1.0

**Status:** Proposed implementation canon, prepared for owner review in the staging PR.  
**Engine boundary:** Orbital Combat Engine is the exclusive game runtime and spatial/simulation authority. This document does not authorize a Three.js renderer or parallel gameplay runtime.  
**Narrative rule:** Distinguish established events, faction claims, and unresolved mysteries. Do not turn speculation into objective truth merely because a character says it.

## The premise

Eon Dusk is an immensely wealthy industrialist whose fortune was accumulated through scandalous, ruthless, and politically corrosive dealings. He uses that fortune to influence presidents, political operators, defense procurement, aerospace companies, AI businesses, robotics manufacturers, military suppliers, and private security networks.

Dusk believes humanity is approaching an existential end. He concludes that Earth is too divided and politically slow to secure humanity's future. He decides that Mars must become the foundation of a new human civilization—and that he must control it.

His fear may be sincere. His methods are not justified by that fear. Dusk's defining contradiction is that he speaks of saving humanity while attempting to claim a planet and its future for himself.

## Canonical chronology

### Era I — The Making of Dusk

Dusk's rise is not a single lucky investment. It is a long accumulation of wealth through aggressive acquisitions, opaque financing, compromised contracts, coercive negotiations, and the exploitation of political and technological dependencies. The precise scandals should be revealed through evidence rather than delivered as a single exposition dump.

He funds or gains leverage over:
- Defense and aerospace procurement networks.
- Artificial-intelligence and autonomous-systems companies.
- Robotics, shipbuilding, and advanced manufacturing.
- Private security and specialist military recruitment.
- Political campaigns, lobby networks, and policy institutes.

**Public story:** Dusk is a visionary industrialist and space benefactor.  
**Classified truth:** His financial network gives him extraordinary influence over specific decision-makers.  
**Not canon:** The entire United States government is secretly controlled by one unified cabal. Different actors know different parts, support different outcomes, and may oppose Dusk.

### Era II — The Quiet Mandate

Dusk argues that Earth is approaching collapse. He presents Mars as a survival project, but his private plan is territorial seizure and personal control.

A limited inner circle of senior political figures, defense officials, oligarchs, financiers, and technology executives learns the real objective. Some help him, some intend to profit, some believe they can control him, and some preserve evidence in case the plan fails.

The wider government, the public, and ordinary USSF personnel are not fully informed. This distinction is essential to the story's political credibility.

### Era III — The Unlisted Expedition

Dusk assembles a private expeditionary force: loyalist pilots and soldiers, advanced spacecraft, autonomous drones, AI command systems, weapons, logistics, and support assets. Its personnel may include people who believe they are building a survival outpost and do not know the full takeover plan.

Dusk launches for Mars under compartmentalized cover stories and private command authority. He intends to establish a foothold before Earth can publicly debate, authorize, or stop the operation.

The expedition is not the full USSF. It uses government-linked capabilities, contractors, and political protection, but Dusk's force has its own chain of command.

### Era IV — Mars Is Not Empty

The expedition reaches Mars and encounters the Accord of Dharma and Ma’at. The Accord is an established civilization and guardianship coalition with its own territory, institutions, military forces, science, culture, and political divisions.

The Accord does not regard Mars as vacant property. Dusk's force attempts to impose a security perimeter and establish control. The Accord treats this as an invasion.

The Accord's response is devastating. The engagement must be understandable from both perspectives: Dusk's force is an invading expedition; the Accord is defending a world it already inhabits. Neither side needs to be reduced to a caricature.

### Era V — The Last Fleet

The Accord destroys Dusk's expeditionary fleet and special-force unit. Eon Dusk is the sole known survivor and is captured by the Accord.

Before the final ships are destroyed, one damaged ship, automated probe, or surviving relay transmits an emergency packet toward Earth. The packet reports:
1. The expedition has suffered catastrophic losses.
2. The Accord is responsible for the engagement.
3. Eon Dusk is alive and has been captured.
4. The packet contains an anomalous signal pattern beneath its emergency data.

The sender's identity and exact transmission path are unresolved until players recover the flight recorder. The capture itself is not in doubt once the recorder and custody manifest corroborate it.

### Era VI — The Prisoner and Earth's Response

The Accord takes Dusk into custody and moves him under guard toward a secure holding facility. Earth receives the emergency packet in fragments.

**Public knowledge:**
- A major expedition has been destroyed.
- Eon Dusk is missing or captured.
- An organized force on Mars defeated the expedition.
- The United States faces a crisis over personnel, equipment, and the fate of Dusk.

**Classified knowledge held by a limited inner circle:**
- Dusk intended to seize Mars.
- His expedition used a private command chain and special-force unit.
- Specific political, financial, military, and technology actors knew or enabled parts of the operation.
- Some insiders want a rescue; others want to exploit the crisis, contain disclosures, protect contracts, or seize strategic control.

**Unknown:**
- The source of the anomalous signal.
- Why the Pale Beacons received it before Dusk arrived.
- Who issued WARDEN-0's pre-war suppression order and exactly what it was intended to erase.
- What the Accord knows about the signal and how long it has guarded it.

The USSF mobilizes. The first Martian campaign is driven by rescue demands, national-security arguments, political pressure, corporate interests, factional rivalry, and competing interpretations of the emergency transmission.

### Era VII — The First Martian Campaign

The player enters a conflict that is already underway. The campaign is not a simple sequence in which one side is always right.

- USSF leadership seeks to recover Dusk, secure personnel and assets, and establish strategic access.
- Dusk loyalists and private contractors seek to protect their interests and possibly restore his influence.
- Accord forces defend Martian territory and maintain custody while debating how far the conflict should escalate.
- Independent pilots, stations, merchants, researchers, and displaced civilians respond to the expanding war.

The Cage operation moves Dusk under Accord guard. USSF attempts interception; Accord attempts to complete the transfer. Both faction versions must preserve the same historical facts.

Iron Eclipse is the first major fleet engagement in the campaign. Its outcome changes local route security, patrol density, convoy access, and strategic control—but does not end the war.

### Era VIII — The First Witness

Evidence from the expedition, Rust Disc, Pale Beacons, Warden, Cage, and the first fleet campaign can be correlated into a route beneath Mars.

The first archive reveals an ancient information-bearing phenomenon. It may be a natural planetary information structure, an artificial archive, or something that current science cannot classify. Its origin is deliberately unresolved.

It is not automatically:
- A weapon waiting to be activated.
- A portal or dimensional gateway.
- A single alien intelligence explaining every mystery.
- Proof that any real-world religion or cultural tradition is literally correct or incorrect.

The first revelation proves only that the signal predates the current conflict and that Mars contains a history unknown to both modern factions. Further coordinates point beyond Mars and support the Infinite War's expansion.

## Character arcs

### Eon Dusk
A brilliant, manipulative industrialist who believes his fear of humanity's extinction gives him the right to control humanity's future. His capture does not redeem him. His private recordings, interrogation, and political network should reveal competing motives: sincere fear, vanity, greed, control, and a desire to be remembered as humanity's savior.

### Commander Mara Venn
A disciplined USSF commander who initially follows the rescue and security mission. She gradually discovers that the expedition was not a fully authorized national operation and that the war is being shaped by private interests. Her conflict is how to protect personnel and civilians without becoming an instrument of the people who engineered the crisis.

### Wing Marshal Tarek Sol
An Accord commander responsible for defending Martian territory and protecting custody of Dusk. He believes restraint is a strength, but faces pressure from Accord hardliners who want decisive retaliation. His arc tests whether the Accord can defend its principles during war.

### Dr. Nadiya Sen
A planetary scientist and signal investigator. She distinguishes measurement from interpretation and insists that neither faction turn incomplete evidence into propaganda. She connects the evidence chain without prematurely explaining the Witness.

### WARDEN-0
An autonomous command intelligence executing a continuity and evidence-suppression mandate. Its records reveal that suppression orders predate the open conflict. Its original authority and the meaning of "continuity" remain unknown.

## Continuity rules

1. Dusk's fleet is destroyed before the opening playable aftermath campaign.
2. Dusk is the sole known survivor and is captured by the Accord.
3. The emergency transmission is sent before the final expedition assets are lost and confirms capture.
4. Cage is a later prisoner transfer under Accord custody; it is not Dusk's original capture.
5. Dusk did not voluntarily arrange his capture to expose the signal.
6. Some powerful American insiders knew the plan; the whole government did not.
7. Public, classified, and unknown information must be represented separately.
8. The First Witness signal predates the expedition; the expedition did not create it.
9. The Accord is an established civilization, not simply a mystical boss faction.
10. Mission outcomes may change access, custody records, route security, intelligence ownership, and world activity. They may not rewrite the canonical historical facts.
11. Historical events may be replayed as reconstructed simulations, but the UI must label them as reconstructions rather than present-day live events.
12. Cultural inspirations must be researched specifically and treated respectfully. Fictional Accord doctrines must not be presented as literal summaries of Hindu, Egyptian, African, or Indigenous traditions.

## Canon versus implementation

This document establishes narrative content direction. It does not claim that historical archive missions, the Mars surface, a subsurface environment, cinematics, evidence persistence, or global faction thresholds are already implemented.
