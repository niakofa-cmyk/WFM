# WAR FOR MARS — Player Journey Direction

This is the product direction for the initial game and future improvements. The original user-provided concept is preserved at `attached_assets/Pasted-This-is-a-match-based-game-and-players-first-meet-onlin_1792518148921.txt`.

## North-star journey

Build and validate the complete player journey, not a growing collection of disconnected screens:

1. **Understand the conflict and choices.** On opening the game, players should understand the war over Mars, the USSF and the Accord of Dharma and Ma'at, and the available single-player and online modes.
2. **Choose and prepare a ship.** Players select a faction-appropriate craft, understand its role, and configure it before launch.
3. **Fight clearly and responsively.** A 3D battle should feel engaging and readable, with controls, objectives, combat feedback, and outcomes that make sense.
4. **Trust the match.** Single-player and online play should behave consistently. Online matches must use authoritative state rather than trust a modified client.
5. **Keep earned progress.** Match and mission results should be validated, awarded once, and saved to the player's account.
6. **Use rewards meaningfully.** Credits and discoveries should support purchases, ship refits, technology upgrades, and access to new options.
7. **Return to a changing universe.** Travel, stations, exploration, side missions, and faction choices should give players meaningful reasons to keep exploring and replaying.

## Initial game identity

- Combat is the central activity; economy, campaign, and exploration should support and deepen it.
- Offer both a single-player route and 5v5 online combat. Preserve isolated match rooms and clear online status; do not imply matchmaking or a shared live galaxy exists until it is built.
- The conflict between the USSF and the Accord of Dharma and Ma'at is the narrative frame. Mars, the missing expedition, and Eon Dusk's capture provide the opening stakes.
- Ship identity should come through readable roles, silhouettes, weapons, and abilities. The original concept's Mandjet, Shakuna, Sundara, and Tripura are foundational inspirations; the current roster and balance process continue to evolve.
- Stations should be useful places to receive communications, choose missions, trade, and refit. Exploration should lead to discoveries, encounters, and new choices rather than only marking map locations complete.
- The player-driven market and persistent universe are long-term goals. Keep development claims aligned with what is actually implemented and verified.
- Treat the setting and combat as speculative fiction. Do not present unverified claims about real-world classified vehicles, weapons, or capabilities as fact.

## How to use this direction

For a proposed feature or polish pass, ask which step of the journey it improves and how that improvement will be observed or tested. Prefer connecting existing systems into a working loop over adding standalone screens. Keep code verification, local network simulation, deployed-network evidence, and physical-device testing clearly distinguished.
